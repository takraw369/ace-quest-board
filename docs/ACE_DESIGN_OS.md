# ACE Design OS

## Purpose

ACE should not feel like a dashboard with a theme. It should feel like entering a coherent world whose visual language supports self-observation, direction, practice, growth, and reflection.

The design goal is not decoration. The design goal is to make the user feel, without explanation:

- I know where I am.
- I can see where I am heading.
- I know the next move.
- My actions leave traces.
- I am growing inside a living system.

## Research-first operating rule

Do not design ACE from taste alone.

For any meaningful redesign, the design agent should work in this order:

1. Define the user, context, screen purpose, and dominant task.
2. Research current best-in-class products and relevant design systems for specific strengths.
3. Decompose references into transferable principles: hierarchy, navigation, interaction, motion, feedback, accessibility, visual rhythm, and world-building.
4. Rebuild those principles for ACE rather than copying surface styling.
5. Prototype the smallest high-fidelity surface.
6. Test clarity, emotional response, and next-action comprehension.
7. Promote only stable patterns into the ACE Design OS.

MASA should not need to become a UI designer. The AI/design system carries the research burden; MASA supplies worldview, taste judgment, lived experience, and final directional feedback.

## Design learning curriculum

ACE design research should continuously learn across six layers, in this order:

1. **Product UX**
   - What job is the user trying to finish?
   - What must be obvious in the first few seconds?
   - What friction is intentional vs accidental?

2. **Information architecture**
   - What is primary, secondary, contextual, and hidden?
   - Can users understand where they are and what comes next?
   - Can a feature live inside an existing surface instead of becoming a new URL?

3. **Visual hierarchy**
   - Typography, spacing, grouping, contrast, density, progressive disclosure.
   - Simplicity means focus, not merely removing decoration.

4. **Motion and feedback**
   - Motion should explain state changes, causality, continuity, and completion.
   - Never add motion simply to make the interface feel premium.
   - Important information must remain understandable without animation.

5. **World-building**
   - Light, atmosphere, language, illustration, sound, material, spatial metaphor, and seasonal/day-night variation.
   - World-building must reinforce behavior and identity rather than obstruct usability.

6. **Accessibility and real-life use**
   - Legibility, tap targets, reduced-motion support, color independence, cognitive load, one-handed use, outdoor/low-light conditions.
   - Accessibility is part of product quality, not a late compliance pass.

## Emotional objective

ACE should create **ready curiosity**: grounded enough to sense clearly, alive enough to want to move.

Not hype. Not spa-like passivity. Not fantasy-game noise.

The desired emotional movement is:

`settle -> sense -> choose -> act -> reflect -> grow`

Every major screen should reinforce one of these verbs.

Day and night may express different moods while preserving the same behavioral logic:

- Day: openness, sky, air, possibility, exploration, movement.
- Night: mystery, moonlight, story, immersion, reflection, quiet anticipation.

A day/night shift must never change navigation logic or make the product harder to use.

## Design genes

ACE combines five design genes:

1. **Nature / Biotope**
   - living systems, seasons, terrain, circulation, recovery, growth
   - use as spatial and behavioral metaphors, not decorative leaf icons

2. **Japanese restraint**
   - ma, asymmetry, quiet hierarchy, material texture, selective emphasis
   - avoid pseudo-traditional ornament and stereotyped "Japanese" motifs

3. **Athlete clarity**
   - timing, next action, progress, recovery, feedback
   - action screens must be fast to read under physical or cognitive load

4. **Quest / Journey**
   - routes, checkpoints, choices, discoveries, milestones
   - gamification should reveal growth, not create compulsive reward loops

5. **OS / Intelligence**
   - the system connects state, direction, learning, action, people, and history
   - intelligence should feel calm and contextual rather than technical or chat-bot-heavy

## World model

The ACE world is not a menu tree. It is a living journey.

### HOME = Camp / Today
Where am I today? What matters now? What is the next move?

### ME = Inner Map
Current state, calibration, direction, identity-in-motion, growth history.

### QUEST = Trail
One concrete action at a time. Clear start, effort, completion, reflection.

### LEARN = Library / Dojo
Knowledge appears because it helps the current phase, not because a catalog exists.

### PEOPLE = Encounter
People, coaches, peers, places, and opportunities appear contextually when they can change the user's trajectory.

These names are conceptual design metaphors. The UI does not need to literally label every area as Camp, Trail, or Dojo.

## Visual direction

### Base palette

Use a natural foundation with light and warmth as signals of life and direction.

Day can use:
- sky / mist: openness and possibility
- leaf / moss: regulation, life, recovery, connectedness
- sand / paper: reflection, memory, knowledge
- sun / amber: direction, active focus, center pin

Night can use:
- indigo / deep night: immersion and focus
- moon / lantern gold: orientation, warmth, next move
- jade / moss: continuity with daytime ACE identity
- paper / warm neutral: reflection and knowledge

Color should encode meaning consistently. Do not add colors merely to make screens feel richer.

### Light

Light is a primary world-building device.

- current focus can appear as a source of warmth
- completed or integrated elements can become subtly brighter or clearer
- overload or ambiguity should reduce contrast rather than turn everything red
- day/night transitions may shift atmosphere without changing information hierarchy

### Texture

Use texture sparingly to avoid sterile SaaS flatness:

- paper grain for reflection or knowledge surfaces
- subtle topographic, star, path, or radial field lines for direction and flow
- soft atmospheric gradients for state transitions

Never reduce legibility for texture.

## Typography

Use typography to separate reflection from execution.

- reflective / philosophical phrases: a Japanese-capable serif with strong readability
- action, metrics, buttons, navigation: a clean sans-serif
- large text should be rare and meaningful

Avoid making every heading poetic. Operational information must remain operational.

## Shape language

- containers: soft but not bubbly
- buttons: confident, simple, action-oriented
- progress: paths, arcs, nodes, rings, or terrain-derived structures are preferred over generic percentage bars when comprehension remains strong
- cards should not become the default answer to every information problem

The interface should use space and grouping before adding borders.

## Motion

Motion should communicate state change, direction, causality, and completion.

Good uses:

- a Quest becomes active
- a calibration shifts
- a route opens
- a reflection closes a loop
- progress accumulates over time
- day transitions into night without relocating controls

Motion should feel physical: settle, open, connect, pulse, flow.

Avoid casino-style celebration, constant particles, fast blinking, decorative parallax, and movement without meaning.

Support reduced-motion preferences and never rely on animation as the only carrier of important information.

## Information hierarchy

Every primary screen should answer one dominant question.

### HOME
1. How am I now?
2. What matters today?
3. What do I do next?

### QUEST
1. What is the action?
2. Why now?
3. How small can I make the first move?
4. What happened after doing it?

### LEARN
1. Why is this relevant to me now?
2. What is the one idea to understand?
3. What changes in practice?

### ME
1. What is changing in me?
2. What direction am I choosing?
3. What patterns are emerging over time?

## Want to / Direction design

Want to should not be presented primarily as a large database.

The default experience should surface:

- 1 Center Pin
- up to 3 active directions
- evidence from recent behavior
- optional deeper exploration

A large Want to library remains an advanced editing and reflection tool.

The user should be able to move from:

`I want this -> this matters because -> this is my current distance -> this is today's smallest move`

without opening multiple apps or remembering multiple URLs.

## World-building through language

World-building is partly copywriting.

Prefer language that feels embodied and directional:

- current position
- next move
- trail / route
- bottleneck
- recovery
- direction
- rhythm
- phase
- trace
- reflection

Avoid excessive jargon, invented nouns, or RPG terminology that users must learn before they can act.

## Reference principles

Study products for specific strengths, not for visual copying.

Reference categories to continuously research:

- emotional-state products: atmosphere before interaction
- performance products: clarity under action and cognitive load
- learning products: visible progression and adaptive guidance
- games / exploration products: anticipation, route, discovery, world continuity
- Japanese digital products / editorial systems: restraint, hierarchy, material sensitivity
- platform design systems: navigation, accessibility, motion, input conventions

Examples may include Calm, Nike Run Club, Duolingo, Headspace, Apple platform patterns, and other current best-in-class products, but references must be periodically refreshed rather than becoming permanent idols.

ACE should synthesize principles through its own philosophy, athletic roots, nature/biotope model, and Japanese restraint.

## Reference capture format

For every useful reference, capture:

- product / screen / source
- target user and context
- what emotion it creates in the first 3 seconds
- dominant task
- information hierarchy
- interaction / motion pattern
- what specifically works
- what would fail for ACE
- transferable principle
- ACE translation idea
- screenshot / visual reference when available

This turns inspiration into reusable design intelligence instead of a moodboard graveyard.

## Anti-patterns

Do not ship:

- generic SaaS dashboard layouts with ACE-colored cards
- gold-on-black luxury styling as a substitute for identity
- samurai, shrine, brush-calligraphy, or Zen stereotypes without functional meaning
- Arabian or Japanese ornament used as costume rather than meaningful world-building
- RPG inventory / badge overload
- charts because data exists
- five competing CTAs on one screen
- separate URLs that users must remember for each conceptual layer
- motion that competes with content
- tiny controls, low contrast, or beautiful-but-unreadable typography

## Design quality test

Before shipping a screen, ask:

1. What is the single question this screen answers?
2. What emotion should the user have after 3 seconds?
3. What is the one next action?
4. Does the visual hierarchy reveal that without reading instructions?
5. Does this feel like ACE if the logo is removed?
6. Is any decoration competing with action or understanding?
7. Does it work first on a phone, one-handed, in imperfect real-life conditions?
8. Does it remain understandable with motion reduced?
9. Are controls comfortable to tap and text comfortable to read?
10. Which researched principle justifies each non-obvious design decision?

If question 5 is "no", the world-building is not strong enough yet.
If question 10 cannot be answered, the design is probably taste-driven rather than evidence-informed.

3. Redesign HOME around `State -> Direction -> Next Quest` using the learned principles.
